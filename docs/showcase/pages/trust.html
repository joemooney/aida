02 / EVIDENCE AT THE POINT OF ACTION

A record you can follow.
Checks that can stop the work.

SEPARATION

A different reviewer.

The normal review path separates the change's author from its reviewer. AIDA checks reviewer attribution rather than treating the implementer's summary as approval. A restricted, explicit solo-human exception exists.

SPECIFICITY

The exact commit.

Verdicts are recorded against a reviewed head. The merge decision considers the current change and applicable evidence, instead of assuming an old approval covers new code.

AUTHORITY

Limits that remain.

Grants, mandates, merge holds and protected operations govern what may proceed. A dark-factory execution mode does not override those boundaries.

04 / CURRENT PRODUCT BOUNDARIES

Clear limits are part of the product.

Alpha, Linux only.

Supported release targets are Linux x86_64 and arm64. Broader-team scale and unattended reliability remain under validation.

One Unix-user trust boundary.

Multi-human identity separation under a shared Unix user is not a security boundary. Do not treat role labels as tenant isolation.

Harnesses differ.

Claude Code, Codex and Antigravity do not offer identical launch, permission or containment capabilities. Check support for the selected path.

Merge is not deployment.

Production deployment needs its own policy and automation. Recorded review evidence is not a compliance certification or proof of defect-free software.

Local records need care.

Canonical requirements, derived caches and local review/dispatch evidence have different backup and synchronization needs.

Model access is separate.

A local store does not make a network-dependent coding agent air-gapped. Vendor authentication and model access remain operator responsibilities.
